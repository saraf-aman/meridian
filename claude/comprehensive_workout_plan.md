# BACK SAFETY FRAMEWORK

> **This section applies to every single session across all three phases. Read it before your first session and review it if you ever feel any unusual sensation in your back.**

---

## Understanding Your Injury

Based on your description — a pain that felt like a single nerve running from below the shoulder blade down to the lower centre of the back — this is most consistent with a **paraspinal muscle strain or thoracolumbar fascial irritation**. The good news: this pattern is less likely to be a disc issue (which typically radiates into the leg, not upward along the spine). It is more likely a muscular or connective tissue injury that has fully healed over the past 2 years.

However, since it was never formally diagnosed, we treat it as an ongoing risk factor. The approach is simple: **build the supporting structures around the spine before loading it**. This is what Phase 1 is entirely designed to do.

---

## Permanently Excluded Until Further Notice

These exercises are off the table until Phase 3 and only after the back has been tested progressively through Phase 2:

- Barbell back squat with heavy load
- Conventional deadlift with heavy load
- Good mornings (barbell)
- Hyperextensions with added weight
- Jefferson curls
- Any exercise that combines spinal flexion with rotation under load
- Sit-ups or crunches with added weight

---

## Exercises Requiring Extra Caution (do them, but go light and slow)

- Romanian deadlift — hip hinge only, zero rounding of the lower back, stop at mid-shin
- Dumbbell bent-over row — must maintain neutral spine throughout
- Plank and its variations — stop if you feel any compression or aching in the lower back
- Seated cable row — do not round forward at the end of the movement

---

## Red Flag Symptoms — Stop Immediately If You Feel Any of These

| Symptom | What to do |
|---|---|
| Sharp shooting pain anywhere along the spine | Stop the exercise. Do not continue the session. Rest 48 hours minimum. |
| Tingling or numbness down either leg | Stop immediately. Do not return to training. See a doctor before next session. |
| The same nerve-like sensation you felt during your original injury | Stop immediately. This is a direct warning. Rest and assess. |
| Lower back pain that persists more than 30 minutes after a session | Do not train the next day. If it continues past 48 hours, see a doctor. |
| Pain that gets worse as the session progresses | Stop the session. Mild DOMS-type ache improving during warm-up is normal. Pain that builds is not. |

---

## Green Light Sensations (Normal — Do Not Panic)

- Mild muscle soreness in the lower back 24–48 hours after a session — this is DOMS, it is normal
- A feeling of tightness in the back after a long sitting day — warm up slowly and it will ease
- Mild fatigue in the back muscles after core exercises — this is the muscles working, not injury

---

## The Core-First Principle

Every session in Phase 1 includes dedicated core stability work. This is not optional and is not filler. The dead bug, bird-dog, plank, and glute bridge are the foundation of a back-safe training life. They train the muscles that protect your spine under load. Do not skip them. Do not rush through them.

# HOW TO READ THIS PLAN

---

## Tempo Notation

Tempo is written as **4 digits: Eccentric – Pause at bottom – Concentric – Pause at top**

| Phase | Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Lowering the weight | First number | Count this many seconds going DOWN or OUT |
| Pause at the bottom | Second number | Hold for this many seconds at the end point |
| Lifting the weight | Third number | Count this many seconds going UP or IN |
| Pause at the top | Fourth number | Hold for this many seconds at the top |

**Example: 3-1-2-0**
- Lower the weight for 3 seconds
- Pause 1 second at the bottom
- Lift the weight for 2 seconds
- No pause at the top, go straight into the next rep

**Why tempo matters:** Slowing down the lowering phase (eccentric) is where most muscle is built. It also dramatically reduces injury risk because you are in control of the weight at all times. Do not skip the tempo — it feels harder than just moving the weight up and down, and that is exactly the point.

---

## Sets and Reps Notation

**3 × 10** means: 3 sets of 10 repetitions

A **set** is one complete group of repetitions performed without stopping.
A **rep** (repetition) is one complete movement — for example, pressing the weight up and returning it down is one rep.

---

## Rest Period

The rest period is the time you sit or stand quietly between sets. Use your phone timer. Do not skip rest — it directly affects performance on the next set.

- **60 seconds** — lighter exercises, isolation movements
- **90 seconds** — compound movements (working multiple muscle groups)
- **120 seconds (2 minutes)** — introduced in Phase 2 for heavier compound lifts

---

## Weight Selection Guidance

**In Phase 1, the rule is: start lighter than you think you need to.**

Use this test: if you can complete all prescribed reps with the stated tempo and good form, and you feel like you could do 2–3 more reps, the weight is correct. If you finish the set and could easily do 5+ more, go slightly heavier next session. If your form breaks down before the last rep, the weight is too heavy — drop it.

**Starting weight recommendations are given for each exercise.** These are estimates for someone at your current conditioning level. They are not prescriptive — they are your starting point. Adjust based on how the first session feels.

---

## Progressive Overload Rule (Phase 1)

**The two-session rule:** If you complete all sets and all reps with good form and correct tempo in two consecutive sessions, increase the weight by the smallest increment available (usually 5 lbs on machines, 5 lbs on dumbbells).

Do not increase weight just because one session felt easy. Two sessions in a row confirms it is genuinely manageable, not just a good day.

## Phase 1 Warm-Up Guide

> **There are two warm-up versions: Evening (weekday gym) and Morning (Saturday cardio). They are different because your body is in a different state.**

---

### Evening Warm-Up (Monday, Wednesday, Friday)

Your body has been active all day — muscles are already warm from normal daily movement. The goal here is **joint mobilisation and muscle activation**, not raising body temperature from scratch.

**Duration: 8–10 minutes total**

**Step 1 — Treadmill Walk (3 minutes)**
- Speed: 4.5–5.5 km/h — a purposeful walking pace, slightly faster than casual
- Incline: 0%
- Purpose: gets blood moving to the legs, hips, and core without taxing the system

**Step 2 — Mobility Circuit (5–7 minutes, perform in this order)**

Do each movement for the stated repetitions before moving to the next. This is not a rest circuit — move through it at a steady pace.

| Movement | Reps | How to do it |
|---|---|---|
| Arm circles (forward) | 10 each arm | Stand tall. Extend one arm fully. Draw large circles forward from the shoulder. Keep the movement smooth and large. |
| Arm circles (backward) | 10 each arm | Same as above, reverse direction. |
| Shoulder cross-body stretch | 5 each side, 5 sec hold | Bring one arm straight across your chest. Use the other hand to gently press it closer to your body. Feel the stretch in the rear shoulder. |
| Neck rolls (half circle only) | 5 each side | Drop your chin to your chest. Slowly roll your head to one shoulder, hold 2 seconds, return to centre. Never roll the neck backward — only front to side. |
| Hip circles | 10 each direction | Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, hands on hips. Draw large circles with your hips as if hula-hooping. |
| Leg swings (front to back) | 10 each leg | Hold onto a wall or machine for balance. Swing one leg forward and back like a pendulum. Keep the movement controlled — not a full kick. |
| Leg swings (side to side) | 10 each leg | Same support. Swing the leg across your body and then out to the side. |
| Bodyweight squat (slow) | 8 reps | Feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward. Lower slowly over 3 seconds, pause briefly at the bottom, stand back up. This is a movement rehearsal — no weight. |
| Glute bridge (bodyweight) | 10 reps | Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor. Push your hips toward the ceiling, squeeze your glutes at the top, lower slowly. Wakes up the glutes and activates the lower back safely. |
| Dead bug (slow) | 4 each side | Lie on your back, arms pointing to the ceiling, knees at 90 degrees in the air. Slowly lower your right arm overhead and your left leg toward the floor simultaneously. They should hover just above the floor. Return. Switch sides. Breathe out as you extend. Keep your lower back pressed firmly into the floor throughout. |

---

### Morning Warm-Up (Saturday Cardio)

Your body is coming off sleep. Your spine is slightly compressed from lying down, your muscles are cold, and your joints need more time to prepare. This warm-up is longer and more focused on waking the spine up before you ask it to support any movement.

**Duration: 12–15 minutes total**

**Step 1 — Slow Walk (5 minutes)**
- Speed: 4.0–4.5 km/h — a gentle comfortable pace
- Purpose: raise core temperature slowly, decompress the spine

**Step 2 — Spinal Mobility Sequence (5 minutes, floor-based)**

Do this sequence on a mat if available, or on the floor.

| Movement | Reps/Duration | How to do it |
|---|---|---|
| Cat-cow stretch | 10 reps | On hands and knees. Arch your back upward (cat — like a scared cat), hold 2 seconds. Then dip your belly downward and lift your head gently (cow), hold 2 seconds. Move slowly. This gently mobilises every spinal segment. |
| Child's pose | 30 seconds hold | From hands and knees, sit your hips back toward your heels, extend your arms forward on the floor, let your forehead rest on the mat. Breathe deeply. Gently decompresses the lower spine. |
| Lying knee-to-chest | 5 each side | Lie on your back. Pull one knee gently toward your chest with both hands. Hold 10 seconds. Switch. Releases the lower back and hip flexors. |
| Glute bridge (bodyweight) | 12 reps | Same as above. More reps in the morning because the glutes are slower to activate after sleep. |

**Step 3 — Activation Circuit (5 minutes)**

| Movement | Reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bodyweight squat (slow) | 10 reps | 3 seconds down, pause, stand up |
| Leg swings front/back | 10 each leg | As described above |
| Arm circles | 10 forward, 10 back, each arm | As described above |
| Hip circles | 10 each direction | As described above |

### Detailed Exercise Instructions

---

#### 1. Dumbbell Flat Bench Press
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 10 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-0 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 20–25 lbs each hand

**What this works:** Chest (pectorals), front shoulder (anterior deltoid), back of the upper arm (triceps)

**Equipment:** Flat bench + two dumbbells of the same weight

**How to do it:**
1. Sit on the flat bench with a dumbbell on each thigh. Lean back and kick both thighs up simultaneously to bring the dumbbells to chest level
2. Lie flat on the bench. Hold the dumbbells at chest level, elbows at about 45 degrees from your body — not flared straight out to the sides
3. Press both dumbbells upward and slightly inward, counting 2 seconds. Stop before full elbow lockout
4. Lower slowly, counting 3 seconds, until the dumbbells are at chest level and you feel a slight stretch across the chest
5. Keep your feet flat on the floor and your lower back gently in contact with the bench — do not arch up

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- Do not shrug your shoulders toward your ears during the movement. Keep your shoulders pulled down and back throughout
- Do not arch your lower back off the bench trying to push more weight. Your back stays flat against the bench
- Do not bounce the weight — the controlled return is where most of the muscle is built

---

#### 2. Seated Cable Row
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 10 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-1 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 25–35 lbs

**What this works:** Middle back (rhomboids, mid-trapezius), lats, rear shoulders, biceps

**Equipment setup:**
- This is the cable machine with a low pulley — the cable comes from near the floor
- Attach the **V-bar attachment** (also called a close-grip row handle — it looks like a narrow V or triangle shape with two handles close together). If a V-bar is not available, use a single straight bar or individual rope handles
- Set the weight on the weight stack
- Sit on the bench facing the cable machine. Place your feet on the foot pads or the frame of the machine. Knees slightly bent — not fully extended, not deeply bent
- Reach forward and grip the V-bar handle with both hands

**How to do it:**
1. Start position: sitting upright, arms extended forward holding the handle, cable is taut (not slack). Your torso is at roughly 90 degrees — not leaning back, not leaning forward
2. Pull the handle toward your lower abdomen/navel area. As you pull, drive your elbows backward and slightly downward, as if you're trying to put your elbows in your back pockets
3. At the end of the pull, your hands should be touching or nearly touching your stomach. Your elbows should be behind your body. Squeeze your shoulder blades together for 1 second
4. Return the handle slowly toward the machine, counting 3 seconds. Keep your spine neutral — do not round your back forward as your arms extend
5. Stop before the weight stack touches down. Keep tension on the cable

**Back safety note:** The key danger here is rounding the lower back at full arm extension. As your arms extend forward, your natural tendency will be to lean forward and round. Resist this. Your torso stays upright throughout the entire set. If you cannot keep your back flat, the weight is too heavy.

---

#### 3. Dumbbell Shoulder Press
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 10 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-0 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 10–12 lbs each hand

**What this works:** Shoulders (all three heads of the deltoid), upper back, triceps

**Equipment setup:**
- Pick up two dumbbells of the same weight from the dumbbell rack
- Sit on an adjustable bench set to 90 degrees (fully upright). If no adjustable bench is available, you can do this seated on a fixed flat bench with back support, or even seated on the edge of a bench with your back braced
- Bring the dumbbells up to shoulder height. To get them into position safely: hold both dumbbells at thigh level while seated, then use your thighs to kick them up one at a time to shoulder height

**How to do it:**
1. Start position: dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing forward, elbows at roughly 90 degrees, elbows slightly in front of your body (not flared directly to the sides)
2. Press both dumbbells upward and slightly inward so they come close together at the top (but do not clank them together). Count 2 seconds on the way up
3. At the top, arms are nearly extended — stop before locking the elbows
4. Lower slowly, counting 3 seconds, returning to the start position
5. Pause 1 second at the bottom before the next rep

**Back safety note:** Do not arch your lower back as you press. This is a very common mistake — people arch the back to push more weight. Keep your lower back gently pressed against the bench pad. If you feel yourself arching, the weight is too heavy.

---

#### 4. Lat Pulldown
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 12 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-0 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 35–45 lbs

**What this works:** Latissimus dorsi (the large muscles of the mid-back that give the V-taper shape), biceps, rear shoulders

**Equipment setup:**
- This is the cable machine with the high pulley — the cable comes from above
- Attach the **straight wide bar** (the long horizontal bar that is typically already attached to this machine)
- Set the weight on the weight stack
- Sit facing the machine. Adjust the thigh pad so it sits snugly on top of your thighs when seated — it should hold you down when you pull. This is important: without the pad holding you, your body will just lift off the seat when you pull
- Reach up and grip the bar with both hands. Use an overhand grip (palms facing away from you). Your grip width: slightly wider than shoulder-width. Hands should be at roughly the point where the bar starts to angle downward

**How to do it:**
1. Start position: sitting upright, arms extended above, holding the bar. You will have a very slight backward lean — about 10–15 degrees back. This is correct. Do not lean all the way back like you're lying down
2. Pull the bar down toward your upper chest (top of the sternum area). As you pull, think of driving your elbows downward and back toward your hips. Your chest should rise slightly to meet the bar
3. The bar should come to just below your chin, touching or nearly touching the top of your chest
4. Squeeze the back muscles for 1 second at the bottom
5. Return the bar upward slowly, counting 3 seconds. Let your arms extend fully at the top and feel the stretch in the lats before the next rep

**Common mistakes:**
- Do not pull the bar behind your head. This places significant stress on the cervical spine. Always pull to the front (to your chest)
- Do not use momentum — leaning back aggressively and swinging. The movement comes from the back, not from throwing your body weight

---

#### 5. Dumbbell Bicep Curl
**Sets:** 2 | **Reps:** 12 | **Tempo:** 3-0-2-1 | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Starting weight:** 10–12 lbs each hand

**What this works:** Biceps (front of the upper arm)

**Equipment setup:**
- Stand with a dumbbell in each hand, feet hip-width apart
- Arms hanging at your sides, palms facing forward (supinated grip)

**How to do it:**
1. Keeping your upper arms pinned to your sides and completely still, curl both dumbbells upward toward your shoulders, counting 2 seconds on the way up
2. At the top, your forearms are nearly vertical. Pause 1 second and squeeze the bicep
3. Lower slowly, counting 3 seconds, back to full arm extension
4. Do not swing — your upper arms and elbows should not move at all during the curl. If they are moving, the weight is too heavy

**Option:** You can do these alternating (one arm at a time) or both at the same time. Both at the same time is fine for Phase 1.

---

#### 6. Cable Tricep Pushdown
**Sets:** 2 | **Reps:** 12 | **Tempo:** 3-0-2-1 | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Starting weight:** 12–18 lbs

**What this works:** Triceps (back of the upper arm)

**Equipment setup:**
- Cable machine with the high pulley (same machine as the lat pulldown, but you'll be standing in front of it)
- Attach the **rope attachment** (it looks like a thick rope with two knotted ends). If rope is not available, use a straight short bar
- Set the weight
- Stand facing the machine, feet hip-width apart. Step close to the machine — you should be roughly 30–40 cm from the pulley

**How to do it:**
1. Grip the rope with both hands, palms facing each other (neutral grip). Pull the rope down so your elbows are bent at roughly 90 degrees and your upper arms are pinned to your sides
2. This is the start position: elbows bent, upper arms still, hands in front of your hips
3. Push the rope downward, extending your arms. As you reach the bottom, pull the two ends of the rope slightly apart (outward) — this increases tricep contraction. Count 2 seconds
4. Pause 1 second with arms extended, squeezing the triceps
5. Return slowly upward, counting 3 seconds. Do not let the weight pull your elbows forward or upward — keep upper arms still throughout

---

#### 7. Dead Bug
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 8 each side | **Tempo:** Slow and controlled | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Weight:** Bodyweight

**What this works:** Deep core muscles (transverse abdominis), hip flexors, coordination. This is one of the most important back-safety exercises in the plan.

**Equipment setup:**
- Exercise mat on the floor, or the gym floor if no mat available
- No equipment needed

**How to do it:**
1. Lie on your back. Press your lower back firmly into the floor — there should be no gap between your lower back and the floor. This position must be maintained throughout every rep
2. Raise both arms straight toward the ceiling, wrists above shoulders
3. Raise both legs so knees are at 90 degrees in the air — shins parallel to the floor. This is your starting position
4. Breathe out fully
5. Slowly lower your RIGHT arm overhead toward the floor, and simultaneously lower your LEFT leg toward the floor (straightening it as you go). Move both limbs slowly — take 3–4 seconds
6. They should hover just above the floor (1–2 cm). Do NOT let them touch the floor
7. Return both limbs back to start position slowly
8. Switch: lower LEFT arm and RIGHT leg simultaneously
9. That is one rep on each side — total 8 per side per set

**The most important rule:** If your lower back lifts off the floor at any point during the movement, you have gone too far. Shorten the range of motion. The lower back must stay flat and in contact with the floor at all times. This is what is protecting your spine.

### Detailed Exercise Instructions

---

#### 1. Leg Press (Machine)
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 12 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-0 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 65–90 lbs

**What this works:** Quadriceps (front of the thigh), glutes, hamstrings

**Equipment setup:**
- Sit in the leg press machine. It has a reclined seat and a large platform you push with your feet
- Adjust the seat position: you want your knees at roughly 90 degrees when your feet are on the platform. If your knees are higher than 90 degrees (deep bend), move the seat back. If your legs are almost straight at the starting position, move the seat forward
- Place your feet on the platform at shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward (about 15–20 degrees). Feet should be in the middle of the platform vertically — not at the very top or very bottom
- Set the weight using the pin on the weight stack

**How to do it:**
1. Release the safety handles (there are usually two handles on the sides that you turn to disengage the lock — always check these before starting)
2. Lower the platform toward you by bending your knees slowly, counting 3 seconds. Lower until your knees are at roughly 90 degrees — thighs roughly parallel to the floor
3. Pause 1 second at the bottom
4. Push the platform away by extending your legs, counting 2 seconds. Do not fully lock your knees at the top — leave a very slight bend
5. Do not let the weight stack touch down between reps

**Back safety note:** Keep your lower back and tailbone pressed firmly into the seat pad throughout. A very common mistake is allowing the lower back to round and the tailbone to lift off the seat at the bottom of the movement. This places significant compressive stress on the lumbar spine. If this is happening, you are going too deep — reduce the range of motion.

---

#### 2. Goblet Squat (Dumbbell)
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 10 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-0 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 15–20 lbs dumbbell

**What this works:** Quadriceps, glutes, inner thighs, core. This is the foundational squatting movement that prepares you for barbell squats later.

**Equipment setup:**
- Pick up one dumbbell. Hold it vertically with both hands cupped around the top end of the dumbbell (like holding a goblet — hence the name). Your hands form a diamond shape around the weight
- Stand with feet slightly wider than shoulder-width, toes pointed outward at about 30 degrees

**How to do it:**
1. Hold the dumbbell at chest height against your sternum. Elbows should point downward
2. Before squatting, take a breath in and brace your core
3. Lower your body by pushing your knees outward (in the direction your toes are pointing) and sitting your hips straight down, counting 3 seconds. Keep your chest upright — do not lean forward
4. Lower until your thighs are parallel to the floor, or as low as you comfortably can without your heels lifting or your lower back rounding
5. Pause 1 second at the bottom
6. Push through your heels to stand back up, counting 2 seconds. Breathe out on the way up
7. At the top, do not lock your knees — keep a very slight bend

**Common mistakes:**
- Heels lifting off the floor — this means your ankles are tight. Do not force depth. Go only as low as your heels stay flat
- Chest collapsing forward — keep the dumbbell close to your chest and think "chest up" throughout
- Knees caving inward — push your knees outward actively throughout the movement

---

#### 3. Seated Leg Curl
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 12 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-1 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 25–35 lbs

**What this works:** Hamstrings (back of the thigh)

**Equipment setup:**
- This machine has a seat with a pad that sits on top of your thighs (to hold you down) and a roller pad that sits against the back of your ankles/lower shins
- Sit in the machine. Adjust the thigh pad so it sits snugly on top of your thighs
- Adjust the ankle pad so it sits against the back of your lower leg just above the heel. You should not be able to pull your legs back without the pad engaging
- Adjust the starting position lever so your legs start at roughly full extension (most machines have a lever on the side for this)

**How to do it:**
1. Grip the handles on the sides of the seat
2. Curl your legs downward by bending your knees, pulling the ankle pad down and back, counting 3 seconds
3. At the end of the movement, your knees should be bent at roughly 90 degrees or slightly more
4. Pause 1 second at the bottom, squeezing the hamstrings
5. Return your legs to the extended position slowly, counting 2 seconds
6. Pause 1 second at the top with legs extended before the next rep

#### 4. Leg Extension
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 12 | **Tempo:** 3-0-2-1 | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Starting weight:** 25–35 lbs

**What this works:** Quadriceps (front of the thigh)

**Equipment setup:**
- This machine has a seat with a roller pad that sits against the front of your lower leg just above the ankle
- Sit in the machine. Adjust the back pad so you sit comfortably. Adjust the ankle pad so it sits against the front of your lower leg just above the ankle
- Your knee should align with the pivot point of the machine (the axis it rotates around). Most machines have a marker for this

**How to do it:**
1. Grip the handles on the sides of the seat
2. Extend your legs upward by straightening the knees, pushing the ankle pad up and forward, counting 2 seconds
3. At the top, legs are fully extended. Pause 1 second and squeeze the quadriceps
4. Lower slowly, counting 3 seconds, back to the starting position
5. Do not let the weight stack touch between reps

**Note on back safety:** This is a seated exercise with no spinal loading. It is completely safe for your back. Simply maintain normal upright posture in the seat.

---

#### 5. Calf Raise (Machine)
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 15 | **Tempo:** 2-1-2-1 | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Starting weight:** 35–45 lbs

**What this works:** Calves (gastrocnemius and soleus)

**Equipment setup:**
- If your gym has a standing calf raise machine: stand on the platform with the shoulder pads resting on your shoulders. Balls of your feet on the edge of the platform, heels hanging off
- If your gym does not have a dedicated calf raise machine: use the leg press machine with a light weight. Place just the balls of your feet on the bottom edge of the platform and push through your calves

**How to do it:**
1. Starting position: heels hanging below the platform level (full stretch on the calves)
2. Push through the balls of your feet to raise your heels as high as possible, counting 2 seconds
3. At the top, pause 1 second — really squeeze the calf at the top
4. Lower slowly, counting 2 seconds, back below the platform for a full stretch
5. Pause 1 second at the bottom before the next rep

**Why the full range matters:** Calves respond better to full range of motion than partial bouncing movements. Take the time to get the full stretch at the bottom and the full contraction at the top on every single rep.

#### 6. Glute Bridge (Bodyweight)
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 15 | **Tempo:** 2-1-2-1 | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Weight:** Bodyweight

**What this works:** Glutes (backside), hamstrings, lower back stabilisers. One of the most important back-protective exercises in the entire plan.

**Equipment setup:**
- Exercise mat on the floor
- No equipment needed

**How to do it:**
1. Lie on your back on the mat. Knees bent, feet flat on the floor. Feet are hip-width apart, placed close enough to your backside that if you reach your hands along the floor, your fingertips just touch your heels
2. Arms flat on the floor at your sides
3. Push your hips upward toward the ceiling by squeezing your glutes. Count 2 seconds on the way up
4. At the top, your body should form a straight line from your knees to your shoulders. Squeeze your glutes hard at the top. Pause 1 second
5. Lower your hips slowly, counting 2 seconds
6. Pause 1 second at the bottom (hips just touching the floor) before the next rep. Do not fully relax at the bottom — maintain slight tension

**Important:** Drive upward through your heels, not your toes. If your hamstrings are cramping, it means your feet are too close to your backside — move them slightly further away.

---

#### 7. Bird-Dog
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 8 each side | **Tempo:** Slow and controlled | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Weight:** Bodyweight

**What this works:** Core stability, lower back extensors, glutes, shoulder stability. Specifically designed for back injury prevention.

**Equipment setup:**
- Exercise mat

**How to do it:**
1. Start on hands and knees (quadruped position). Hands directly below shoulders, knees directly below hips
2. Spine is neutral — neither arched nor rounded. Look at a spot on the floor about 30 cm in front of your hands
3. Brace your core gently — think of creating a rigid frame between your hips and ribs
4. Slowly extend your RIGHT arm forward (reaching toward the wall in front of you) and your LEFT leg backward (reaching toward the wall behind you) simultaneously. Count 3–4 seconds to reach full extension
5. At full extension, your extended arm and leg should be roughly parallel to the floor. Hold for 2 seconds
6. Return to the starting position slowly
7. Switch: LEFT arm and RIGHT leg
8. That is one rep on each side

**The critical rule:** Do not let your hips rotate. When you extend your leg, the natural tendency is for the same-side hip to hike upward. Resist this — both hip bones should point straight at the floor throughout. If you cannot prevent hip rotation, reduce the range of your leg extension.

#### 2. Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 10 | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-0 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 15–20 lbs each hand

**What this works:** Hamstrings, glutes, lower back (as a stabiliser). This is the foundational hip-hinge movement.

**Back safety note — read before attempting:** This is the most back-relevant exercise in Phase 1. It is safe and beneficial when done correctly. The key is understanding what a hip hinge is. A hip hinge is NOT a squat and NOT a forward bend with a rounded back. It is a backward push of the hips while the spine stays perfectly neutral. If at any point you feel any sensation in your back beyond mild muscular effort, stop the set.

**Equipment setup:**
- Two dumbbells of the same weight
- Stand upright holding a dumbbell in each hand, arms hanging at your sides, palms facing your body

**How to do it:**
1. Stand with feet hip-width apart. Soft bend in the knees — not deeply bent, just slightly unlocked
2. Before moving, brace your core. Take a breath in and hold it for the duration of the rep
3. Push your hips backward as if you're trying to touch the wall behind you with your backside. As you do this, your torso will hinge forward and the dumbbells will travel down the front of your legs
4. The dumbbells should stay close to your legs throughout — almost dragging against your thighs, then shins
5. Lower until you feel a stretch in the back of your thighs (hamstrings). For most people at this stage, this will be around mid-shin. Do not force it lower. Stop as soon as you feel any tension leaving your hamstrings and moving into your lower back — that is the limit
6. Count 3 seconds on the way down
7. Pause 1 second at the bottom
8. Drive your hips forward to return to standing, counting 2 seconds. Think of pushing the floor away rather than lifting up with your back
9. Breathe out on the way up

**The most important cue:** Your back must remain in a neutral position throughout — a very slight natural arch, not flat and not rounded. If you look at yourself from the side, your back should look exactly the same at the bottom of the movement as it did at the top. The moment your lower back rounds, you have gone too far.

#### 3. Push-Ups
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** Maximum reps with good form (target 8–15) | **Tempo:** 3-0-2-0 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Weight:** Bodyweight

**What this works:** Chest, front shoulders, triceps, core

**Two versions — choose based on current strength:**

**Full Push-Up:**
1. Start in a high plank position: hands slightly wider than shoulder-width, arms fully extended, body in a straight line from head to heels. Feet together or hip-width apart
2. Lower your chest toward the floor, counting 3 seconds. Elbows should travel at roughly 45 degrees from your body — not straight out to the sides, not pinned to your sides
3. Lower until your chest is 2–3 cm from the floor
4. Push back up, counting 2 seconds
5. Do not let your hips sag toward the floor — keep your core braced throughout

**Knee Push-Up (if full push-ups are not yet possible):**
1. Same as above but knees are on the floor. From your knees to your shoulders, your body should still form a straight line — do not put your backside in the air
2. Hands slightly wider than shoulder-width
3. Lower your chest toward the floor using the same technique and tempo

**Start with knee push-ups if you cannot do at least 5 full push-ups with correct form. As soon as you can complete 10 knee push-ups with correct form and tempo, transition to full push-ups.**

#### 4. Dumbbell Bent-Over Row
**Sets:** 3 | **Reps:** 10 each side | **Tempo:** 3-1-2-1 | **Rest:** 90 seconds | **Starting weight:** 15–20 lbs

**What this works:** Middle back, lats, rear shoulder, biceps

**Back safety note:** The bent-over position requires your back to work as a stabiliser. Keep the weight light enough that your back stays completely flat throughout. If you feel the lower back fatiguing before the back muscles you're trying to work, the weight is too heavy.

**Equipment setup:**
- One dumbbell
- A flat bench or a sturdy low platform

**How to do it:**
1. Place your right knee and right hand on the bench. Your right hand is below your right shoulder, right knee below your right hip. Left foot is planted firmly on the floor
2. Pick up the dumbbell with your left hand. Your left arm hangs straight down toward the floor, palm facing your body
3. Your back should be parallel to the floor — flat and horizontal. Look at a spot on the floor directly below your face
4. Pull the dumbbell upward toward your hip, driving your elbow backward and upward, counting 2 seconds. Your elbow should brush past your body and finish behind your hip
5. At the top, pause 1 second. Squeeze the back muscles
6. Lower the dumbbell slowly, counting 3 seconds, back to the fully extended arm position
7. Complete all reps on the left side, then switch
8. When switching: right hand holds the dumbbell, left knee and hand on the bench

#### 5. Plank Hold
**Sets:** 3 | **Duration:** 20–30 seconds | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Weight:** Bodyweight

**What this works:** Entire core, shoulders, back stabilisers

**How to do it:**
1. Start in a forearm plank: forearms on the floor, elbows directly below your shoulders. Fists or flat palms — either is fine
2. Lift your hips off the floor so your body forms a straight line from head to heels. Do not let your hips sag down or stick up into the air
3. Feet hip-width apart, toes tucked
4. Hold for the stated duration. Breathe normally — do not hold your breath
5. Keep your glutes squeezed and your core braced throughout

**Back safety note:** If you feel compression or discomfort in your lower back during the plank, your hips are likely sagging. Try to lift your hips slightly. If discomfort persists, stop and move to the bird-dog instead.

**Progression:** Start with 20 seconds. Once you can hold 30 seconds with perfect form for all 3 sets, you will progress this to 40 seconds in Phase 2.

---

#### 6. Mountain Climbers (Slow)
**Sets:** 2 | **Reps:** 20 total (10 each leg) | **Tempo:** Controlled — 2 seconds per rep | **Rest:** 60 seconds | **Weight:** Bodyweight

**What this works:** Core, hip flexors, cardiovascular system mildly

**How to do it:**
1. Start in a high plank position (same as push-up starting position): hands below shoulders, body in a straight line, arms fully extended
2. Bring your right knee forward toward your right elbow, counting 2 seconds
3. Return it to the starting position slowly, counting 2 seconds
4. Bring your left knee forward toward your left elbow, counting 2 seconds
5. Return. That is 2 reps (1 per leg)
6. Continue alternating slowly and controlled

**Note:** These are SLOW mountain climbers — not the fast cardio version. The tempo is 2 seconds forward, 2 seconds back per leg. The purpose is core stability, not cardio. If your hips bounce or your lower back sags, slow down further.

### Weeks 1–2: Brisk Walk Protocol

**Duration:** 25–30 minutes total
**Location:** Outdoors (preferred) or treadmill

**What to do:**

**Heart rate guidance:** If you have no heart rate monitor, use the "talk test": you can speak in full sentences, but wouldn't want to hold a long conversation. This is the correct zone.

### Weeks 3–4: Interval Walk Protocol

**Duration:** 28–32 minutes total
**Structure:**
1. 5-minute easy warm-up walk at 4.5–5.0 km/h

3. 5-minute easy cool-down walk

**Total active interval time:** 6 × 3 minutes = 18 minutes plus warm-up and cool-down

**If 1 minute fast walking feels too easy by Week 4:** increase the fast interval to 1.5 minutes and reduce the easy interval to 1.5 minutes. This is your bridge into Phase 2 running.

# PHASE 1 PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD RULES

---

## The Two-Session Rule

**Condition for increasing weight:** You complete ALL sets and ALL reps with correct form and correct tempo in TWO CONSECUTIVE sessions for that exercise.

**How much to increase:**
- Machines (leg press, lat pulldown, leg curl, leg extension): increase by **5 lbs**
- Dumbbells: increase by **5 lbs** (go from 15 lbs to 20 lbs, 20 lbs to 25 lbs, etc.)

**What to do if you cannot complete all reps in a session:**
- Stay at the same weight. Do not decrease it unless you cannot complete more than 60% of the prescribed reps
- If you consistently cannot complete all reps after 3 sessions at the same weight, check your form — the issue is usually technique, not strength

**What to do if you increase the weight and your form breaks down:**
- Drop back to the previous weight immediately
- Spend one more session at the lower weight focusing purely on form
- Attempt the increase again

---

## Rep Ranges in Phase 1

All exercises in Phase 1 use fixed rep targets. The goal in Phase 1 is not to train to failure — it is to build movement patterns, connective tissue, and habit. Training to failure (where you physically cannot do another rep) is a Phase 2 and 3 concept.

**You should always feel like you have 2–3 reps left in the tank at the end of each set.** If you feel like you have 6+ reps left, the weight is too light. If you feel like you barely made the last rep and your form was breaking down, the weight is too heavy.

# MISSED SESSION PROTOCOL

> **This is one of the most important parts of the plan. Read it now, before you ever miss a session, so you have a pre-decided response ready.**

---

## Missed One Day

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Missed Monday (Upper Body) | Do Monday's session on Tuesday. Move Tuesday cardio to Wednesday morning before work if possible, or skip it for this week only. Wednesday lower body stays as scheduled. |
| Missed Wednesday (Lower Body) | Do Wednesday's session on Thursday. Keep Friday as scheduled. |
| Missed Friday (Full Body) | Do Friday's session on Saturday morning (your cardio day). Cardio becomes a rest day this week. |
| Missed a cardio day | Simply skip it. Do not try to double up cardio with a gym session. One missed cardio session has zero impact on your progress. |

**The rule:** Missing one session is completely normal. It does not require guilt, self-criticism, or compensation. Just shift and continue.

---

## Missed Two or More Consecutive Days

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Missed Monday and Tuesday | Start the week's sessions from Wednesday. You will only complete Lower Body and Full Body this week. That is fine — two sessions is better than zero. |
| Missed an entire week (illness, travel, life) | Do not attempt to catch up or compress the missed sessions. Simply resume from where you were. If you missed Week 2, start Week 3 as planned. The week is gone — accept it and move forward. |
| Missed two full weeks or more | Drop back one phase checkpoint. If you were in Week 3, restart from Week 1. Not because you've lost all progress — you haven't — but because easing back in protects your body from injury after a break. |

---

## The All-Or-Nothing Trap — Read This

The most dangerous pattern is not missing a session. It is the thought that follows: *"I've already broken the streak, so I might as well skip the rest of the week too."*

**This thought is the enemy.** One missed session followed by three more sessions is a normal week. One missed session followed by a two-week gap is where the habit dies.

**Pre-decided rule:** The maximum gap between any two sessions is 5 days. If it has been 5 days since your last session, the next session happens no matter what — even if it's a 20-minute lighter version.

---

### The Barbell Squat — Detailed Form Guide

**Equipment setup:**
- Barbell on a squat rack. The squat rack has two uprights (vertical poles) with adjustable hooks called J-hooks
- Set the J-hooks at approximately your shoulder height when standing. The bar should sit at a height where you can unrack it with a slight bend of your knees, not with a very deep squat down
- Set the safety bars (the horizontal bars lower down on the rack) at a height just below where your hips would be if you were at the bottom of a squat. These catch the bar if you fail a rep
- Starting weight: just the barbell. An Olympic barbell weighs 45 lbs. If this feels heavy at first, look for a lighter technique bar (25–35 lbs) which some gyms have
- Apply collars (the clips on the ends of the bar) even with just the bar or light plates

**Bar position — high bar (Phase 2 starting position):**
The bar sits on the shelf of muscle at the top of your upper back — just below the bony bump at the top of the spine. It should NOT be on the bony part of the spine and should NOT be on your neck. The position feels awkward at first. Practice without weight.

**How to unrack:**
1. Stand under the bar, position it on the upper back shelf
2. Grip the bar with both hands slightly wider than shoulder-width
3. Brace your core, take a breath in and hold it
4. Stand up to lift the bar from the hooks
5. Take 2 controlled steps backward — one step back with the right foot, one step back with the left. Stop

**How to squat:**
1. Feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed outward 20–30 degrees
2. Take a deep breath in and brace your core as if bracing for a punch — this is called the Valsalva maneuver and it protects your spine
3. Push your knees outward in the direction your toes are pointing
4. Lower by pushing hips back and down, counting 3 seconds. Keep your chest up — do not fold forward
5. Lower to parallel (thighs parallel to the floor). Do not go deeper in Phase 2
6. Pause 2 seconds at the bottom
7. Drive up through your heels, counting 2 seconds. Breathe out on the way up
8. At the top: do NOT lock your knees fully. Maintain tension

**How to re-rack:**
1. Walk forward (2 small steps) toward the rack after completing your set
2. Find the hooks by feel — do not look down
3. Lower the bar onto the hooks

**Back safety note:** If at any point the weight causes you to lean significantly forward (your chest dropping toward your thighs), stop the set. This is too heavy. In Phase 2, the barbell squat is a form-building exercise — not a maximum effort exercise.

---

### The Conventional Deadlift — Detailed Form Guide

**Starting weight:** Barbell only (45 lbs). No exceptions. The deadlift is the highest-risk exercise for your back history. We approach it with full respect.

**Equipment setup:**
- Load the barbell on the floor. With just the bar and no plates, the bar will be very close to the floor. Add a pair of 10 lb plates to each side to raise it to the correct starting height (mid-shin to just below knee)
- The correct starting height for a deadlift bar is approximately mid-shin

**Setup position:**
1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward
2. Walk up to the bar. The bar should be over the mid-foot — not against your shins, not far away. Roughly 3–5 cm from your shins
3. Bend down and grip the bar with both hands just outside your legs — double overhand grip (both palms facing you). Hands should be just outside shoulder-width
4. Without lifting the bar, set your back: lower your hips until your shins touch the bar. At this point, your hips should be above your knees, your shoulders should be above the bar (slightly in front of it), and your back should be in a neutral position (slight arch, same as standing)
5. Take a deep breath in and brace your core hard
6. Create tension: before you pull, take the slack out of the bar by pulling upward gently until you feel resistance. This prevents the bar from jerking off the floor

**The pull:**
1. Push the floor away (think of a leg press movement — push the ground down with your feet) while simultaneously pulling the bar against your legs. The bar should travel in a perfectly vertical line — dragging against your legs the whole way
2. As the bar passes your knees, drive your hips forward to stand up
3. Count 3 seconds on the way up (it will feel slow at this light weight — that is correct)
4. At the top: stand tall, hips fully extended, shoulders slightly back. Do NOT hyperextend your back (lean backward)
5. Pause 2 seconds at the top
6. Return the bar to the floor by reversing the movement: push hips back first, then lower the bar along your legs, maintaining neutral spine throughout

**Back safety rule:** If you feel any sensation in the area of your original injury during or after the deadlift, stop immediately. Do not continue the set. This exercise is introduced with extreme conservatism. Any back signal is a stop signal.

# EXERCISE FORM LIBRARY

This section is a quick-reference form summary for every exercise across all phases.

---

## Core Principles for Every Exercise

1. **Neutral spine:** Unless an exercise specifically requires a different position, your spine should always maintain its natural curves — slight forward curve in the lower back, slight backward curve in the upper back. Never fully flat, never aggressively arched, never rounded.

2. **Core bracing:** Before every heavy set, take a breath in and brace your abdominals as if bracing for a punch. This creates intra-abdominal pressure that protects the spine. Release at the top of the movement, breathe, re-brace for the next rep.

3. **Controlled tempo:** Never drop weights, never use momentum to swing a weight up. The controlled lowering phase is where most muscle damage (the good kind) occurs.

4. **Joint stacking:** Joints should generally be aligned with each other. In a press: shoulder, elbow, and wrist should stack. In a squat: knee should track over the second and third toe.

5. **Grip:** Firm enough to control the weight. Not so tight that your forearms fatigue before your target muscles.

---

## Back-Safe Movement Principles

These apply specifically given your back history:

- **Hip hinge vs lumbar flexion:** A hip hinge (as in Romanian deadlift) keeps the spine neutral while the hips fold. Lumbar flexion (rounding the lower back) is different and problematic under load. In every hinging exercise, the movement comes from the hips, not the lower back.
- **Bracing before loading:** Every exercise that involves the back — even indirectly — requires a braced core before the weight moves.
- **Stopping before end-range under fatigue:** When you are tired, form breaks down. The most common breakdown is lumbar rounding at the end-range of rowing movements and deadlifts. Stop the set if you cannot maintain neutral spine.

# TEMPO & NOTATION REFERENCE

---

## Tempo Quick Reference

| Notation | Eccentric (down/out) | Pause at bottom | Concentric (up/in) | Pause at top |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3-1-2-0 | 3 sec | 1 sec | 2 sec | 0 sec |
| 3-0-2-1 | 3 sec | 0 sec | 2 sec | 1 sec |
| 2-1-2-1 | 2 sec | 1 sec | 2 sec | 1 sec |
| 3-2-2-0 | 3 sec | 2 sec | 2 sec | 0 sec |

**Why a 2-second pause for barbell squats and deadlifts:** The 2-second pause at the bottom eliminates the "bounce" that many people use to get out of the bottom of a squat. Bouncing uses elastic energy from the tendons rather than muscular strength. More importantly for your back history, it removes any sudden loading spike at the bottom of the movement.
